# Vixoria Technologies: first staff augmentation clients in 30 days

**Run dates:** 21 October–19 November 2026 (30 calendar days)

**Owner:** Vijit

**Stretch target:** 3 distinct paying staff augmentation clients by day 30.
**Operating assumption:** Vixoria starts without a proven staff augmentation track record or a standing developer bench. Verify both before claiming either.

## The honest starting point

Three new clients in 30 days is an aggressive target, especially if every prospect is cold. A more defensible base case is **0–1 signed clients and a qualified pipeline**; two is a strong month, and three requires warm introductions or buyers who already have an approved, urgent need. Do the work for three, but do not represent three as a forecast.

For this roadmap, **one closed client** means a *distinct company* has signed a paid developer engagement, named a start date, and paid the agreed first invoice or platform-funded milestone. A conversation, verbal yes, unpaid trial, candidate submission, or three developers at one company does not count as three clients. A paid four-week embedded-developer pilot does count if it meets these conditions.

Vixoria's current website emphasizes projects Vixoria builds, with TrueWorker and WikiSchools as product examples; it does not yet make embedded developers obvious. The existing outreach dashboard already separates **Agency partners** from **Potential clients**. Its October research imported 158 agency contacts: 89 initially ready and 69 requiring more review. That is a research asset, **not** proof of current demand, verified decision-makers, or unsent status. Check live dashboard history before contacting anyone. Sources: [site homepage](index.html), [agency outreach README](outreach-dashboard/README.md), [contact audit notes](outreach-dashboard/research/README-subcontract-contacts.md).

## Why the plan is shaped this way

- In Clutch's survey of 451 B2B buyers, referrals were the most used source for researching and evaluating business services, and 52% spent at least a month researching/evaluating providers. Thus, introductions and buyers already in market offer the best chance of a 30-day close; a new website alone will not do it. [Clutch buyer survey](https://clutch.co/report/b2b-buying-process-how-businesses-purchase-b2b-services-software).
- Clutch's October 2026 analysis of 23,346 verified service-provider reviews found founders and executives more prominent among small-company buyers, while technical leaders become more important at larger software buyers. Thus, contact founders at small agencies and CTOs/delivery leaders at larger ones. The dataset covers service purchases broadly, not Vixoria's exact offer. [Clutch buyer analysis](https://clutch.co/resources/who-buys-b2b-services).
- Instantly reports a 3.43% average **overall reply rate per email sent** in its 2026 platform data, including follow-ups and all types of replies. It is a vendor dataset, not a probability of a positive reply or signed client. At that average, even 200 cold emails would yield only about seven replies, so cold email cannot be the only route to three closes. [Instantly benchmark and definition](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026).
- In one founder's first-person account, it took more than 80 Upwork applications to get a first response, while the new agency's first large contract later came through a referral. This is an anecdote, not a typical conversion rate; it supports running active-job and referral channels in parallel. [Founder story on Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/leading-voices/how-to-start-an-agency).
- Diwali falls on **Sunday, 8 November 2026**. Expect some Indian buyers and developers to be slower around it; get Indian meetings and proposals moving before 7 November and use the festival period for overseas follow-ups and preparation. Actual company closures vary. [India Post holiday list](https://www.indiapost.gov.in/holidays-list).

## The offer: one clear second direction for Vixoria

Keep **project delivery** and **staff augmentation** visibly separate:

| Buyer need | What Vixoria sells | Who directs daily work? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Build this software for us” | A scoped project or managed team | Vixoria |
| “Our team needs another engineer” | A named, interviewed developer for a defined term | Client |

**First offer:** one dedicated .NET/C# or React developer joining the client's existing team for an initial paid four-week period, with an option to continue monthly. Offer part-time only when the candidate and client can both support a clear schedule. Add Angular, QA and other roles only after Vixoria can screen and supply them reliably. The paid pilot makes the first decision smaller; it is still a real engagement, not free work.

**Proposed homepage message:** “Build your software. Extend your engineering team.” Place two actions above the fold: **Discuss a project** and **Add a developer to your team**. Add a dedicated `/services/staff-augmentation/` page and a Services-menu link. Explain skills, how technical screening works, how the client interviews the developer, who manages daily work, expected overlap, and how to request a profile. Use the existing projects as evidence of software delivery, not as invented proof of previous placements.

**One-sentence agency pitch:** “Vixoria helps software teams add a technically screened .NET or React developer to an active project, working inside the team's own tools and delivery process.”

## Who gets priority

### India lane — aim for the fastest first two opportunities

Target founder-led software agencies and product teams, roughly 5–100 people, especially firms with an active .NET/React requirement, client project overflow, or a referral path to the decision-maker. Start with Delhi NCR and existing Vixoria relationships, then expand to other Indian cities where a named buyer and need are visible. A job ad is **evidence of a skill gap**, not evidence that the company accepts vendors; ask whether outside capacity is useful.

### Overseas lane — aim for one fast-moving agency opportunity

Make the **UK the primary overseas test**: 100 of the 165 contacts in Vixoria's October audit are marked UK, although the market label alone does not verify buyer fit or demand. Approach US and Australian agencies during this sprint only when there is a warm introduction, a live brief, or strong partner evidence. Favor smaller agencies with a reachable founder/CTO/delivery lead and willingness to work with India-based developers. Do not spend this 30-day sprint on large-company supplier registration unless an existing contact can move it through quickly. Quote in the client's currency after a requirements call. Confirm work-hour overlap before proposing a person.

### Qualification: pursue now only when these are answerable

1. What role, stack, seniority and hours are needed?
2. Is there an active project or an approved start window within 30 days?
3. Who interviews and signs off? Who owns the budget?
4. Is the buyer open to an external developer working with its team?
5. Can Vixoria name a suitable candidate and a commercially viable price?

If need or budget is absent, put the account into later follow-up rather than counting it as a 30-day opportunity.

## Four acquisition lanes and working targets

These are **activity targets**, not researched conversion guarantees. Count unique companies, not messages.

| Lane | 30-day working target | Why it matters |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Warm network: past customers, ex-colleagues, founders, agency contacts, suppliers | 40–50 personal conversations or introduction requests | Trust and shorter buyer discovery time |
| Audited agency list, with a current need/partner signal checked before outreach | 70–90 distinct agencies | Reuse Vixoria's existing research rather than restart scraping |
| Active briefs on marketplaces or agency partner/application pages | 25–35 relevant responses | Buyer has publicly expressed a need; reject ordinary employment vacancies |
| Indian companies with a relevant public hiring/overflow signal | 30–40 distinct companies | Creates local opportunities and potential warm calls |

Some accounts may overlap; deduplicate them in the dashboard/CRM. On a normal working day after setup, aim for **8–10 well-researched first contacts, 5–8 timely follow-ups, 2–3 warm calls/introduction asks, and up to 2 high-fit active-brief responses**. Never send the same generic message to all 158 imported contacts. The dashboard already has draft history, reply tracking and a deliberate send action; use those controls.

**Stretch funnel for three closes:** 15–20 discovery calls → 8–10 confirmed, funded needs → 6–8 candidate-backed proposals → 3 paid signed engagements. This is reverse planning, *not* a forecast or industry benchmark. It requires unusually good fit and fast buyers. If by day 14 there are fewer than five genuine requirements, three closes by day 30 are highly unlikely; change the target segment or offer rather than just sending more email.

## Daily operating rule

Reserve each workday for (1) new demand, (2) live deals, (3) candidate supply. Reply to interested buyers the same working day; offer two call slots. After every call, send a short written recap with the role, budget range, interview date, proposed start and next owner. Track every account as: **researched → contacted → replied → discovery booked → qualified requirement → candidate proposed → interview → commercial terms → signed → paid**. Separately track “no need,” “wrong contact,” and “later.” Review this board every evening. Do not treat opens or connection accepts as opportunities.

## Messages to use, then personalize

**Warm introduction request**

> Hi [Name], I'm adding a focused service at Vixoria: a screened .NET or React developer joining an existing team for a paid project period. Do you know an agency or product team that has an active capacity gap? If someone comes to mind, an introduction would help. Happy to share the one-page overview.

**Indian agency first contact**

> Subject: Extra .NET/React capacity for [Company]
>
> Hi [Name], I saw [specific current project, service or hiring signal]. I'm Vijit at Vixoria Technologies. We build software and can help an existing delivery team add a screened .NET or React developer for a defined period, working in its own process. Is there an active role or client project where outside development capacity would help? If so, I can discuss the requirement and propose a matching person.

**Overseas agency first contact**

> Subject: Development capacity for [Company]
>
> Hi [Name], I noticed [specific delivery/partner signal]. I run Vixoria Technologies in India. We can supply a .NET or React developer to work within your team's tools and agreed overlap hours, including under a white-label arrangement if useful. Are you open to discussing an active capacity gap? I can share how we screen people and propose a candidate against a real brief.

**Active brief response**

> I read your need for [role] on [project]. The key requirement appears to be [specific point]. Vixoria can propose a developer who has [relevant, verified experience] and can overlap [hours]. Could we confirm [one important unknown] and arrange a short technical interview? I can then send a firm rate and start date.

**Follow-up after 3–5 business days**

> Hi [Name], following up on the [role/project] capacity question. Is adding an external developer relevant this month, or should I leave it for later? If there is a live brief, I can discuss it this week.

For all messages, replace bracketed text with verified facts. Do not claim a ready bench, a guaranteed 48-hour start, prior placements, or a candidate's availability unless true. Upwork's own proposal guide emphasizes answering the buyer's specific need and leading with relevant proof; apply the same principle to direct messages. [Upwork proposal guide](https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-create-a-proposal-that-wins-jobs/).

## Discovery call and closing checklist

Use a 20-minute call: **5 minutes** on the project and why capacity is needed now; **5** on role, stack, hours and team process; **5** on budget, decision-maker, interview and start date; **5** on Vixoria's screening and exact next step. End by booking the candidate interview or explicitly placing the buyer in later follow-up.

A candidate-backed proposal should fit on 1–2 pages: buyer's brief, candidate's verified match, weekly hours and overlap, pilot length, start date, monthly or pilot fee, invoicing/payment schedule, and what happens if the match fails. Quote only after costing the actual person. For a quote floor, use **fully loaded monthly resource cost ÷ (1 − required gross-margin fraction)**; for example, ₹1.4 lakh of direct cost and a *chosen* 30% gross-margin target implies a ₹2 lakh quote. This is arithmetic for internal planning, not a market rate or net profit. If the buyer's budget is below the floor, change scope or walk away.

Common objections:

| Buyer says | Useful response |
| --- | --- |
| “We already have recruiters.” | “Understood. Are you trying to hire permanently, or do you need someone contributing to a current project before a hire is complete?” |
| “We don't know Vixoria.” | Show real work, explain your technical screening, offer a candidate interview and a paid four-week pilot. |
| “Send us your bench.” | “I will share profiles matched to your role after confirming skill level, hours and start date.” |
| “Your price is high.” | Compare the *specific* hours, seniority and support included; offer fewer hours or a narrower paid pilot if viable. |
| “Maybe next quarter.” | Ask for the likely trigger and a date to revisit; remove from the 30-day close forecast. |

## What to measure and when to change course

Track **unique accounts contacted**, warm introductions, live briefs, positive replies, discovery calls held, qualified requirements, candidate interviews, proposals, paid signatures, and cash received **separately for India and overseas**. Record why every deal is lost or delayed. Positive reply means evidence of interest or a useful referral, not “thanks” or an automatic response.

- **Day 10:** If fewer than two actual briefs, the list is too broad or the message too vague. Spend the next two days on warm introductions and active requirements.
- **Day 14:** If fewer than five funded or plausibly funded needs, three closes are no longer a sound expectation for this month. Continue the sprint, but report the likely outcome honestly.
- **Day 22:** If a qualified buyer exists but no interviews/proposals, the bottleneck is candidate supply or pricing; fix that before adding leads.
- **Day 30:** If zero clients close, retain the qualified pipeline and choose one segment/channel based on observed meetings, not email opens. If one closes, prioritize delivery and referrals before scaling outreach.
